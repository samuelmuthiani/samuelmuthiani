    About Samuel

    A calm room, a clear plan, and no judgement.

    I am a counseling psychologist who believes people already hold much of what they need to feel better. My job is to help you hear it, test it, and build habits around it.

    In the first sessions we map what is happening and what you want to change. After that, we work at a pace you can sustain, and we check together that it is helping.

    Everything you share stays confidential, within the limits of safety and the law, which I will explain before we begin.

    • Cognitive behavioural therapy
    • Person-centred counseling
    • Trauma-informed care
    • Grief and bereavement work
    How it works

    From first message to steady progress

    1. Free intro call

      Fifteen minutes to share what brings you here and ask anything about the process.

    2. First session

      Sixty minutes to understand your story, your goals and what has helped before.

    3. Shared plan

      We agree on goals, how often to meet, and how we will know it is working.

    4. Regular sessions

      Usually weekly at first, then spaced out as you gain steadiness. We review together.

    Questions

    Good to know

    Is what I say confidential?

    Yes. I share nothing without your consent, except where someone is at serious risk of harm or the law requires it. I explain this in our first session.

    How do online sessions work?

    You receive a private video link before each session. You only need a phone or laptop, a stable connection and a quiet space.

    How many sessions will I need?

    It varies. Many people notice change within six to twelve sessions. We review your progress regularly and you decide when to stop.

    What if I need to cancel?

    Please tell me at least 24 hours ahead and we will move your slot without charge.

    Can I bring my teenager?

    Yes. A parent or guardian arranges the booking, and we agree early on what is shared with the family.

    Anxiety3 min read5 Oct 2026

    When your mind will not switch off at night

    Why worry gets louder after dark, and a small routine that helps.

    Read the article

    At night there are fewer distractions, so unfinished thoughts get your full attention. That does not mean something is wrong with you.

    Try a ten-minute "worry window" early in the evening. Write down what is on your mind and one next step for each item. When a worry returns later, remind yourself it has a slot and a plan.

    If sleep has been disrupted for more than a few weeks, speak to a professional.

    Grief3 min read28 Sep 2026

    Grief has no schedule

    Why months later can feel harder than the first week.

    Read the article

    Early on, shock and ritual carry you. Later, the practical world resumes and the loss becomes quieter and more real.

    Name what you miss in specific terms: a voice, a routine, a place at the table. Specific grief is easier to share and to carry.

    Birthdays and anniversaries can bring waves. Plan something small for those days and let others know.

    Burnout4 min read19 Sep 2026

    Tired, or burnt out?

    Three signs that rest alone will not fix it.

    Read the article

    Ordinary tiredness lifts after a good weekend. Burnout does not. Watch for dread before work, a growing cynicism, and a sense that nothing you do matters.

    Recovery usually needs changes to workload and boundaries, as well as rest. Start by choosing one commitment to renegotiate this week.

    If your mood has dropped for weeks, a conversation with a counselor can help.
